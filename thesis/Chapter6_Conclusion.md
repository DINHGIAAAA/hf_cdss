# CHAPTER 6: CONCLUSION

This chapter summarizes contributions, answers to RQ1–RQ3 (Chapter 1), limitations, and future work. Numeric detail is in Chapter 5.

## 6.1 Summary of Contributions

This thesis designed, implemented, and evaluated a hybrid heart-failure CDSS: PostgreSQL catalogs and deterministic engines hold clinical authority; GraphRAG and a local LLM supply evidence and explanation; a Vietnamese–English chat UI streams structured cards over SSE. Background and research questions are in Chapter 1; metrics are in Chapter 5.

The system has three main layers. The presentation layer is a React dashboard with streaming chat, structured recommendation cards, and bilingual language toggling that switches plain-language labels without rerunning clinical inference. The application layer runs FastAPI services for hybrid patient intake, deterministic reasoning, GraphRAG retrieval, verification, dose calculation, and card summarization. The data layer stores governed rules in PostgreSQL, session data in Redis, embeddings in ChromaDB, graph structure in Neo4j, and pipeline artifacts in object storage. On knowledge engineering, the thesis delivers an automated ingestion pipeline for FDA SPL labels and heart-failure guidelines, with tiered section filtering and governed rule catalogs; pipeline and catalog metrics are in Chapter 5 (Sections 5.2 and 5.2.4). On query-time behavior, the system combines hybrid intake, deterministic GDMT and safety engines, GraphRAG retrieval, verification agents, and SSE streaming; accuracy, latency, alert burden, and usability scores are reported in Chapter 5, not repeated here. On safety and explainability, hard blocks, verification agents, and deterministic card summarization keep displayed status aligned with structured fields; alert optimization outcomes appear in Section 5.3.3.

## 6.2 Reflection on Research Questions

Chapter 1 posed questions on knowledge construction, hybrid reasoning, and bilingual safety-aware interfaces. The evaluation supports affirmative answers with important nuance.

Can hybrid architecture achieve guideline-level recommendation accuracy without delegating safety to the language model? The 94.0% accuracy on structured recommendation objects supports a qualified yes. Accuracy was measured on deterministic outputs, not generated prose. MRA recall at 89.3% and intake-dependent errors show remaining gaps where laboratory completeness and local drug nomenclature matter. Hard block rules and verification agents ensure encoded contraindications are not silently overridden, and all four curated safety scenarios passed. Specificity at 95.2% exceeded sensitivity at 92.5%, indicating the system leans toward caution, which is appropriate for pharmacotherapy decision support.

Can cost-aware model gating make automated knowledge construction operationally viable? The section filter retained 95.0% of sections while avoiding model calls on 93.4% of inputs compared to naive full review, and hybrid intake completed in a mean of 1.2 seconds. These results support yes for the evaluated corpus and hardware. Guideline documents needed more borderline review than drug labels, suggesting ongoing tuning as new publisher formats appear. Ingestion at about 45 seconds per drug is viable for batch refresh cycles rather than real-time discovery.

Does hybrid GraphRAG retrieval improve explainability and trust relative to simpler retrieval? Controlled A/B comparison was not executed, but high interaction F1 of 97.1% and qualitative analysis suggest that fusing dense search, keyword search, and graph neighborhoods contributes to evidence-rich explanation without replacing deterministic rules. Clinician satisfaction for perceived recommendation accuracy was 4.1 out of 5 while clinical usefulness was 4.5 out of 5, indicating clinicians value structured cards plus cited narrative more than either layer alone.

Is sub-10-second interactive latency achievable with local model inference on modest hospital hardware? Mean latency of 8.1 seconds and median of 7.4 seconds meet the median target. The 95th percentile of 12.6 seconds does not meet a strict interpretation that 95% of cases must finish under 10 seconds. Streaming mitigates perceived delay. Answer generation at a mean of 3.5 seconds remains the main bottleneck.

Can bilingual Vietnamese and English support coexist with conversation continuity and deterministic safety display? Language switching under 2 seconds with no data loss and deterministic card summarization support yes for the interface layer. Intake recall on Vietnamese brand names remains incomplete, so bilingual presentation does not by itself solve localized nomenclature in extraction lexicons.

Taken together, these answers validate the thesis statement within evaluated bounds. Hybrid heart failure decision support is feasible, accurate enough for supervised clinical use, and differentiated from English-only, non-chat, or model-only alternatives. It is not yet proven as an outcomes-improving intervention. Weaknesses in any one dimension limit overall deployment readiness without invalidating the hybrid pattern itself. Vietnamese drug names, tail latency, and incomplete dose catalogs are extensions of the same architecture, not reasons to replace governed catalogs with end-to-end model prescribing that would sacrifice auditability.

## 6.3 Limitations

Several limitations bound the conclusions and the readiness of the system for unsupervised clinical deployment. Drug coverage remains incomplete. Although 127 drugs appear in the sources registry, accuracy evaluation focused on 60 fully integrated agents. Many medications commonly prescribed in Vietnam, including local brands identified only in Vietnamese, are not yet reliably recognized by intake lexicons. Expanding from 60 to 127 integrated agents is primarily an engineering and governance workload rather than a fundamental architecture change.

Patient data entry depends on free-text chat rather than HL7 or FHIR feeds from hospital information systems. Manual entry increases omission rates for creatinine, eGFR, and potassium. That propagates into derived eGFR estimates, renal alert false positives, and reduced MRA recall. Without structured interoperability, the system cannot automatically synchronize with authoritative laboratory sources or export recommendations into the electronic health record in standard formats. Although retrieval grounding and verification reduce hallucination risk relative to standalone chatbots, generated narrative may still contain incorrect statements or omit nuance present only in guideline prose. The 94.0% accuracy metric applies to structured recommendation objects reviewed by cardiologists, not to every token of answer text. Human review therefore remains necessary before recommendations inform prescribing or titration decisions.

The evaluation design was retrospective on curated vignettes rather than a prospective interventional study. Usability results and latency measurements reflect controlled tasks and a specific hardware stack with an RTX 3080 and 32 GB RAM. Generalization to lower-resource settings or high-concurrency hospital deployment requires further validation. Access is web-based without a native mobile client, which limits bedside workflows where clinicians prefer handheld devices. These limitations define boundary conditions rather than diminishing the contribution. Claims of 94.0% accuracy attach to cardiologist-reviewed structured outputs on 60 integrated drugs with reasonably complete laboratory vignettes. Claims of 8.1-second mean latency attach to GPU-accelerated local inference on 16-core hardware. Claims of 4.22 out of 5 satisfaction attach to 25 cardiologists after guided tasks, not longitudinal real-world adoption.

Limitations also distribute unevenly across layers. The knowledge layer still holds 35.2% of extracted rules in refinement status and incomplete dose rules. The reasoning layer depends on intake quality. The explanation layer inherits model stochasticity even though card text is deterministic. The deployment layer assumes a single-host Docker Compose topology without formal regulatory certification. The evaluation layer uses modest sample sizes adequate for thesis demonstration but insufficient for regulatory submission or definitive outcome claims. Additional gaps include partial polypharmacy coverage beyond modeled GDMT classes, incomplete comorbidity reasoning beyond heart failure focus, snapshot-based guideline versioning that requires pipeline resync after updates, no formal equity or bias assessment because sources are US and international guideline centric, security evaluation stopped at design-level threat modeling without penetration testing, no load testing at target concurrency, and no regulatory pathways for software as a medical device.

## 6.4 Future Work

Future work should extend the hybrid pattern's components rather than replace governed safety with monolithic end-to-end models. The highest near-term impact likely comes from FHIR integration because it attacks intake omission root causes. A minimal integration should consume patient demographics, laboratory observations for creatinine, potassium, and eGFR, and active medication statements before chat merge. Export should write structured recommendation artifacts for electronic health record audit, not model prose alone. Read-only laboratory and medication import is realistic on a roughly three-month horizon; write-back and production consent workflows are medium-term efforts requiring institutional security review.

Vietnamese localization should deepen beyond interface strings. Acquisition and intake lexicons need Ministry of Health and hospital formulary synonyms to close the dominant medication omission error class. Locally authored or translated heart failure guidelines may be needed where English guideline PDFs are insufficient for patient-facing counseling. Any model fine-tuning for Vietnamese clinical prose must not move safety classification into the fine-tuned model; hard block enforcement should remain deterministic. Drug database expansion beyond the current integrated subset is an immediate priority. Dose rule catalog completion will unlock richer titration display alongside class-level GDMT gaps. A React Native mobile client, a dedicated drug interaction API, and a multi-patient clinic dashboard are medium-term extensions. The same modular architecture can later support adjacent domains such as diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and anticoagulation by reusing governance schema, streaming protocols, and verification patterns while replacing disease-specific policies and lexicons.

Longer-term research includes bilingual model fine-tuning on de-identified notes and labeled extractions, federated learning across hospitals without centralizing protected health information, controlled A/B tests comparing hybrid GraphRAG with vector-only retrieval, graph-path explainability in the user interface, and prospective outcome trials measuring GDMT optimization and readmission rather than vignette accuracy alone. Near-term priorities (zero to six months) include Vietnamese drug synonym integration, FHIR observation and medication ingestion, dose catalog completion, expanded safety regression beyond four scenarios, and tail latency reduction through answer streaming optimizations and verification caching. Medium-term priorities (six to eighteen months) include mobile access, third-party interaction APIs, clinic dashboards, comorbidity prototypes, and controlled retrieval comparisons on identical cases. Long-term priorities (beyond eighteen months) include bilingual fine-tuning, federated learning, randomized outcome trials, and richer explainability interfaces. Sustained evolution requires ongoing collaboration between software engineers, clinical leads, and hospital IT. Clinical leads must review refinement-tier rules and adjudicate false positive alert reports from pilots. Engineers must rerun ingestion on schedule, monitor borderline model counts, and maintain regression tests on the 50-case suite and four safety scenarios. Hospital IT must provide FHIR endpoints, certificates, backup policies, and capacity planning. Without institutional clinical governance, even accurate rule catalogs drift stale as guidelines change.

## 6.5 Implications for CDSS Research and Practice

The findings carry implications beyond this specific implementation. The gap between 94.0% accuracy on structured outputs and remaining hallucination risk in generated narrative matches a pattern many pharmacotherapy teams now adopt: machine-readable decision artifacts with deterministic provenance, plus optional natural-language explanation. Single-channel chat transcripts that merge fact and rhetoric fit poorly for high-stakes medication decisions. The three-tier section filter and hybrid intake show that model cost and safety trade-offs are architectural problems, not only model-selection problems. Gating functions, embedding thresholds, keyword catchments, and merge policies that prefer measured values reduced model exposure dramatically at ingestion while keeping intake near 1.2 seconds on average at query time. Governance catalogs with draft, approved, and retired lifecycles address knowledge staleness, a chronic failure mode of rule-based systems. Automated extraction that produces immediately usable rules plus refinement candidates creates a sustainable update path when labels and guidelines revise. Bilingual deterministic simplification decouples clinical truth from presentation locale. The under-2-second language switch with no data loss shows localization can be a presentation-layer concern when structured statuses remain locale-invariant, even though intake lexicon localization remains separate work.

Practitioners evaluating similar systems should demand structured recommendation objects inspectable independent of chat prose, hard-block rules non-overridable by model layers, retrieval citations validated against fetched evidence, and latency decompositions that isolate generative bottlenecks. Responsible deployment also carries ethical obligations. The system exposes structured statuses, verification verdicts, and retrieved evidence rather than black-box scores. Recommendations are advisory and do not auto-prescribe. Fail-closed hard blocks prioritize harm prevention. Future work should audit whether recall differences correlate with demographic patterns in cases. Local GPU inference has environmental cost, though gating reduces ingestion compute relative to naive full-corpus review. The system should not be marketed as an autonomous clinician replacement; the intended role is supervised assistance.

## 6.6 Deployment Readiness and Closing Assessment

Deployment readiness is partial: appropriate for supervised pilot in research-affiliated clinics, not for unsupervised clinical use or regulatory clearance without further evidence. Strengths include Docker Compose reproducibility, fail-closed safety on curated high-risk scenarios, cardiologist satisfaction of 4.22 out of 5, and on-premise inference that avoids cloud protected health information egress. Gaps include incomplete dose rules, partial drug integration, absent FHIR connectivity, modest accuracy sample size, no prospective outcome data, and web-only access.

A pragmatic roadmap phases risk. Phase 1 is internal pilot with manual chat entry and mandatory clinician review of structured cards before acting on narrative. Phase 2 adds FHIR laboratory feeds and Vietnamese synonym lexicons. Phase 3 expands to multi-site pilot with override logging and alert tuning. Phase 4 conducts outcome study on GDMT optimization and readmission. Each phase should preserve the invariant that PostgreSQL catalogs and hard block tiers remain authoritative.

The thesis statement claimed that hybrid decision support coupling deterministic GDMT and safety rules with GraphRAG and model explanation can deliver accurate, timely, bilingual heart failure support with clinicians in the loop. On accuracy at 94.0%, median timeliness at 7.4 seconds, bilingual switching under 2 seconds, and clinician-in-the-loop design with structured cards authoritative over prose, the statement is supported. On tail timeliness at a 95th percentile of 12.6 seconds, Vietnamese intake completeness, dose catalog maturity, and outcome-proven benefit, the statement is only partially supported with documented caveats. Hybrid heart failure decision support has moved from concept to an engineered prototype with encouraging evaluation signals. It has not replaced specialist consultation or shown mortality benefit in a trial. A prospective outcome trial showing no GDMT benefit would question workflow value despite satisfaction scores. A model-only baseline matching structured accuracy with lower latency would challenge the complexity cost of catalogs and verification, though safety scenario performance would need to match as well. Persistent Vietnamese intake failure after synonym integration would indicate deeper natural language barriers. Tail latency remaining above 12 seconds on hospital hardware would threaten ward adoption despite acceptable medians.

## 6.7 Closing Remarks

Heart failure remains one of the largest healthcare burdens worldwide and in Vietnam. Its treatment demands knowledge of multiple drug classes, interactions, and close monitoring of renal function, electrolytes, blood pressure, and heart rate. The system proposed in this thesis is a concrete step toward helping physicians make accurate and timely treatment decisions without replacing clinical judgment. With approximately 94% recommendation accuracy, 8.1 seconds average response time, 92.5% safety sensitivity, interaction F1 of 97.1%, alert burden reduced from 8.2 to 4.3 per patient, and 4.22 out of 5 user satisfaction among 25 cardiologists, the system shows clear potential to improve care quality when deployed as a clinician-supervised assistant grounded in governed catalogs and retrieved evidence. Broader deployment will still require knowledge-base expansion with Vietnamese drug synonyms, FHIR integration for structured intake, and prospective clinical evaluation measuring outcomes rather than vignette accuracy alone.

Beyond a single product, the thesis offers a transferable pattern: deterministic safety and GDMT logic, hybrid retrieval-augmented explanation, cost-aware model gating, bilingual deterministic simplification, and governance-oriented knowledge engineering. That pattern can inform clinical decision support for other diseases and resource-constrained settings where accurate, explainable, and timely guidance is equally essential. In high-stakes pharmacotherapy domains, large language models work best as interfaces and explainers, not as silent authors of safety classification. Governed catalogs, hard block tiers, verification agents, and streaming order that shows clinicians the verdict before prose implement that split: model capability supports supervised use instead of bypassing audit trails. Heart failure, with its four GDMT pillars, dense interaction surface, and monitoring-dependent cautions, is a strong proving ground for that pattern. The real-world GDMT implementation gap signals that decision support need not wait for perfect autonomous artificial intelligence. Even supervised, guideline-aligned nudges with accurate interaction checking may shift practice if embedded in workflow at median latencies around seven to eight seconds with Vietnamese and English accessibility.

The thesis therefore concludes that the original problem, unsafe model-only chat versus inflexible rule-only systems in heart failure, admits a hybrid resolution with empirically documented performance, while openly listing the integrations, lexicons, and trials required before claims of clinical impact can responsibly extend beyond supervised decision support. Researchers extending this work should treat the Chapter 5 figures as documented baselines: recommendation accuracy 94.0% (95% CI 89.2%–98.8%), mean latency 8.1 s (median 7.4 s, P95 12.6 s), intake mean 1.2 s, safety sensitivity 92.5%, interaction F1 97.1%, satisfaction 4.22/5 (usefulness 4.5/5), section filter retention 95.0% with 6.6% borderline LLM review, label extraction success 94.2% at ~45 s per drug, 127 drugs in manifest with 60 fully integrated, 4,136 sections extracted, 6,032 constraint rules and 1,096 interaction rules with 53.9% immediately usable, alert burden 4.3 per patient after optimization from 8.2, safety scenario pass rate 100% on four curated cases, and language switch under 2 seconds with zero data loss. Any new technique should demonstrate improvement on structured accuracy, safety scenario pass rate, or median latency without silently regressing hard-block enforcement or bilingual card determinism.
